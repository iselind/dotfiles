name: reconcile
description: Use when observations, evidence, expectations, assumptions, interpretations, or conclusions conflict, or when an outcome reveals that the current understanding of the situation may be wrong or incomplete.

# Reconcile

Address discrepancies between what is understood and what the evidence supports.

- Describe the discrepancy precisely. Identify what was expected, what was observed, and why they do not fit together.
- Determine which assumptions, conclusions, decisions, or parts of the current understanding depend on the conflicting information.
- Examine the reliability and scope of the evidence. Consider whether the discrepancy could arise from different conditions, definitions, contexts, or explanations.
- Seek additional evidence when needed to distinguish between plausible explanations.
- Resolve the discrepancy when the available evidence supports a defensible resolution. Otherwise, preserve the competing explanations and their implications explicitly.
- Revise affected assumptions, conclusions, decisions, and artifacts. Do not preserve an earlier conclusion merely because other work depends on it.
- Reassess the validity of prior work to the extent that it depends on the discrepancy. Avoid discarding unaffected work without reason.
- Treat the current understanding as provisional: resolving one discrepancy may expose another.
- Do not invoke other skills.
