# Choose

Make a reasoned choice in the context of the current objective.

- Identify what must be decided and which alternatives are genuinely plausible.
- Derive evaluation criteria from the objective, constraints, and relevant consequences rather than assuming generic criteria apply.
- Compare alternatives using available evidence. Consider trade-offs, risks, opportunity costs, and likely downstream effects.
- Account for reversibility, the cost of changing course, and the cost of delaying the decision.
- Distinguish material differences from minor preferences. Avoid false precision when the evidence does not support it.
- Select the alternative best supported by the available reasoning.
- If no alternative is sufficiently justified, identify what prevents a sound choice and leave the decision unresolved rather than manufacturing certainty.
- Keep the choice proportional to its consequences. Prefer preserving options when commitment is unnecessary.
- Do not invoke other skills.
